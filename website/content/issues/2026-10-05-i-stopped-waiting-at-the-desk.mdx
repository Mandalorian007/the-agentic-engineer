I drafted this issue standing in a grocery store, ordering shrimp. Voice into a pair of glasses, out to the Mac mini in my closet, and a preview was waiting when I got home.

## What changed in my setup

Even Realities G2. I had no interest in AR headsets, which all felt like strapping hardware to your face. These are glasses first. Small clean display, voice in, a terminal mode with a prebuilt hookup, and an app SDK straightforward enough that I built my own client pointed at my own machine. I've run both. From the outside they look ordinary. Mine have no prescription, though they sell that option.

What I care about is what happens after I talk. One spoken sentence hits `/create-issue-pipeline` and the chain runs without me: templates, structure, humanizer pass, quality review.

## What broke

The failure was me, sitting at a desk.

I'd kick off four agents and then wait, because the next input was mine to give and giving it meant being in the chair. The machines were working and I was the thing holding them up. I lost a lot of hours to being available.

That's a workflow bug, not a hardware one, and going off-desk is just what made it visible. The glasses didn't solve it. I did, by counting how many round trips a session actually needed and then removing them: real entry points instead of step-by-step nudging, and replies short enough to read on four lines while somebody weighs out shrimp.

If your agent asks you three questions before it starts, you're not going anywhere.

## One thing you can paste

Add this to your `CLAUDE.md`. It's the difference between a session you can walk away from and one you can't.

```markdown
## Heads-up sessions

Assume this reply gets read on four lines of a display, in a grocery store.

- Lead with what you did or decided. No preamble, no restating my request.
- Keep it under four lines unless I ask for the long version.
- Don't print code blocks, diffs, or file listings. Name the file instead.
- Ask at most one question, and only when proceeding would be destructive or
  unsafe. Otherwise take the obvious option, state the assumption in a clause,
  and keep going.
- If the work will take a while, say so and start it. Don't wait for a go.
```

That last rule bought back more time than the glasses did.

Reply and tell me what still pins you to your chair. I'm collecting them.
