I recently got asked: "What's one piece of advice for getting started with AI engineering?"

After reflection, my answer is simple: focus on prompting.

Here's why: An LLM is fundamentally text in, text out. The text that goes in—the prompt—controls everything that comes out.

Every custom slash command runs a prompt. Every subagent runs a prompt. Every skill.md file is a prompt. Almost all the advanced AI engineering you see is built on well-crafted prompts.

Prompting is coding in natural language. Just like you learned programming languages, syntax, and architectural patterns, you need to learn:

• How to structure requests
• What formats work
• How to iterate and refine

A prompt written once rarely works perfectly. The skill is in refinement. When you tell an LLM "build a to-do app" with no details, you get poor output. But when your prompt specifies technologies, styling, testing frameworks, and architecture—you get quality results.

This is an always-learning skill. I still refine my prompts constantly. Right before writing this, I edited my image generation prompts to specify isometric views, color schemes, and depth—because I was getting boring outputs.

Master prompting, and you gain a massive advantage over those who just say "do X" and hope for the best.

Start small. Iterate. Study patterns. Build templates. Keep learning.

The prompt is the foundation of everything.
