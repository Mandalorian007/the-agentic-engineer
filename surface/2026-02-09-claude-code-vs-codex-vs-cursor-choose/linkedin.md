Every quarter I sit in a meeting where someone has been asked to pick "the" AI coding tool.

Spreadsheet ready. Demos lined up. Standardization deadline on the calendar.

By end of quarter the spreadsheet is stale, two of the rejected tools have shipped major releases, and the decision is being relitigated.

The question is wrong. Not the answer — the question.

In my latest post I do the honest tool-by-tool take on Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor, then show the question engineering leaders should be asking instead:

→ All three are racing toward generic coding excellence. They will converge.
→ None of them know your domain glossary, your warehouse layout, your auth flow, your release calendar.
→ The leverage is one layer down — MCP servers (your capability layer) and marketplace plugins (your workflow layer) that your platform team builds once.
→ A plugin installed in Claude Code does the same job in Codex, in Cursor, or in whatever ships next. Your platform investment compounds across vendors instead of getting reset every model release.

Tool choice has a half-life of about a model release. Your platform layer has a half-life of years.

If "AI tooling rollout" is on your roadmap this year, the exec question stopped being "which tool do we standardize on?" and became "what's our AI platform strategy, who owns it, and which five plugins ship this quarter?"
