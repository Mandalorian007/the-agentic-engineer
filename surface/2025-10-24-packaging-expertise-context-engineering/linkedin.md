When you start a new Claude Code session, the agent knows absolutely nothing about your codebase. Zero context. No architectural decisions. No patterns. No requirements.

Yet we expect it to just "figure things out."

This is the #1 reason developers get frustrated with AI coding tools. Not because the tools are bad—because we're not packaging our expertise.

Context engineering is the practice of deliberately loading the right information into your AI agent. Think of it as writing the perfect onboarding documentation—but for an AI that reads it every single time.

The most fundamental command: /prime

My /prime command:
1. Lists all git-tracked files (full project map)
2. Reads README (quickstart and workflows)
3. Reads docs/architecture.md (system design)
4. Reads blog-config.yaml (configuration)

In 30 seconds, Claude understands: framework, content format, structure, categories, publishing schedule, and quality requirements.

All without me explaining anything.

The key insight? Treat your agent like a talented but completely inexperienced engineer. Package your expertise into reusable commands. Build workflows on top.

This is agentic engineering: packaging knowledge for reliable, repeatable automation.
